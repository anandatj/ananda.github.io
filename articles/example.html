Why SVPWM and How?

Space Vector Pulse Width Modulation (SVPWM) is an advanced technique for generating sinusoidal voltage ouptut through a digital PWM carrier wave. It provides better DC bus utilization and less harmonic distortion compared to traditional Sinusoidal PWM (SPWM).

The Core Concept

The fundamental principle involves representing the 3-phase voltages as a single rotating space vector in a complex plane. The reference voltage vector \( \vec{V}_{ref} \) is synthesized using the two adjacent active vectors and the zero vectors within a specific sector of the hexagon.

$$ \vec{V}_{ref} \cdot T_s = \vec{V}_1 \cdot T_1 + \vec{V}_2 \cdot T_2 + \vec{V}_0 \cdot T_0 $$

Where \( T_s \) is the switching period, and \( T_1, T_2, T_0 \) are the respective dwell times for each vector state. This method effectively increases the maximum achievable output voltage by roughly \( \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \) (or 15.47%) compared to standard SPWM before overmodulation begins.

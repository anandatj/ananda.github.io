Why SVPWM and How?

Space Vector Pulse Width Modulation (SVPWM) is an advanced technique for generating sinusoidal voltage ouptut through a digital PWM carrier wave. It provides higher DC bus voltage utilization and less harmonic distortion compared to traditional Sinusoidal PWM (SPWM) generation method.

The Concept

The fundamental principle involves representing the 3-phase voltages as a single rotating space vector in the \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) reference frame. The reference voltage vector \( \vec{V}_{ref} \) is synthesized using the two adjacent active vectors and the zero vectors within a specific sector of the hexagon.

$$ \vec{V}_{ref} \cdot T_s = \vec{V}_1 \cdot T_1 + \vec{V}_2 \cdot T_2 + \vec{V}_0 \cdot T_0 $$

Where \( T_s \) is the switching period, and \( T_1, T_2, T_0 \) are the respective dwell times for each vector state. This method effectively increases the maximum achievable output voltage by roughly \( \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \) (or 15.47%) compared to standard SPWM before overmodulation begins.

The advantage of SVPWM is that it can be used to generate sinusoidal voltages with a higher DC bus voltage utilization and less harmonic distortion compared to traditional Sinusoidal PWM (SPWM) generation method. This is achieved by using a single rotating space vector in the \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) reference frame, which results in a higher maximum achievable output voltage compared to traditional SPWM.
